# 01-transit-signage — Transit signage

## Conditions

Wayfinding solved for someone in motion, at a distance, who is deciding, not reading. The sign has
one pass to work: a rider walking at speed, a driver at highway speed, a traveller who may not read
the language printed on the sign at all. The problem is never "communicate this information," it is
"communicate this information in the two seconds before the reader is already past it." A brief
matches this lineage when the real cost of a missed read is a wrong turn taken now, not a detail
missed on a page that can be reread.

## Home vector

`C7 T2 G2 S1 M3 D3` — passes `scripts/check_vector.py` on its own. Chroma is the loud axis at 7,
because the medium's colour system is not decoration, it is the message: a small reserved set of
hues (a line colour, an exit green, a hazard red) each carrying exactly one meaning, read and acted
on before the accompanying word is.

## Hierarchy logic

Colour reserved per category tells the eye what kind of thing it is looking at before it reads
anything. Pictogram and arrow placement then tell it what to do about it. Two devices carry the
whole system: a fixed colour-to-category mapping, and directional symbols positioned to be read as
instruction rather than as label.

Separation between one sign's information and the next is space, not a border and not a shadow —
this is the space-led case in `references/foundations/depth.md`. A physical sign has no room for a
hairline at reading speed; the gap between one instruction block and the next is generous enough on
its own that the eye never mistakes where one ends and the next begins. An interface built from this
lineage holds the same rule: adding a border to shore up a region that reads ambiguously is a sign
the space allotted to it was too small, not a fix for the ambiguity.

## Colour logic

A small fixed set of roles, usually three to five, each locked to one meaning: a route or line
colour, an exit or safety colour, a hazard colour. A neutral, high-contrast ground carries
everything else. The governing rule is exclusivity: no hue may serve two meanings inside the same
system, and a new category earns a new hue only when an existing one truly cannot be stretched to
cover it.

## Rhythm

The interval is generated by the pictogram module itself, not by a baseline grid laid down first.
Stroke weight and symbol bounding box set a unit, and spacing between signs, between a symbol and
its label, and between stacked instructions, recurs as a whole-number multiple of that unit.

## Signature move

One hue, one meaning, held without exception across the whole system. Remove that exclusivity and
the palette turns into ordinary colourful design; the system stops being read at a glance and starts
requiring the label to be read up close, which is the exact failure this lineage exists to prevent.

## What it hands the system for free

Instant category recognition that doesn't depend on reading: colour and pictogram carry the meaning
before the word does, so the system already has a mechanism for cross-language legibility and for
legibility under motion, without inventing either.

## Type character

A geometric grotesque built for legibility at a fixed viewing distance rather than for expression.
One weight does most of the work. Numerals are tabular where they appear at all. Size is set by a
distance formula, not by a display-hierarchy decision — this lineage keeps type flat and lets colour
and symbol carry the hierarchy instead.

## Voice

Plain and terse to the point of near-silence. Words name places and actions, nothing more:
directions, exits, warnings, given as short imperatives. There is no persuasion register here and no
room for one.

## Failure mode

Pushed onto a surface that also needs to carry brand identity, the reserved-hue system collides with
brand colour and the "recognize without reading" property breaks, because a rider can no longer tell
whether a colour means danger or means logo. Pushed past five or six reserved hues, the same
property breaks from the inside: no one can hold that many meanings in memory at a glance, and the
system quietly becomes ordinary decorative colour again.

## What it cancels

Gradient or mood-driven colour, colour as brand expression, type doing rhetorical display work,
and any hierarchy built primarily through size rather than through colour and symbol.

## Behaviour pulled off home

Pull chroma down and the signature move loses its instrument: colour can no longer carry category
meaning, and the system must substitute shape or pattern coding to keep the one-signal-one-meaning
rule alive, at real cost to at-a-glance recognition. Push density up by crowding more categories
into one sign and the colour-reservation rule strains first, since it assumes a small, memorable
category count; past that count the system either drops the rule or stops being legible at speed,
which is the same failure by a different route.
