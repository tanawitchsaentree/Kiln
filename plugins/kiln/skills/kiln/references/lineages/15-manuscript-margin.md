# 15-manuscript-margin — Manuscript margin

## Conditions

A primary text needs commentary running alongside it without either one absorbing the other, and
that commentary accumulates over time from more than one hand rather than arriving finished in a
single pass. The margin is a legitimate second content stream carrying its own authority, not
decoration around the main block and not a footnote demoted out of the way. Match this lineage when
the brief's real problem is two genuinely co-present tracks of content with unequal but both-real
authority, not any layout that merely has a sidebar.

## Home vector

`C3 T6 G6 S3 M1 D6` — no axis reaches 7, so there is no loud axis at home position. Spread passes at
5 (max 6, min 1) but nothing here is a spend; type and grid both sit at 6, high enough to matter but
not high enough to be a declared loud axis under the 7-or-above rule. Like the field guide, this
lineage's interest does not come from spending hard on one axis, it comes from grid complexity: a
primary text block and a marginal apparatus are a real two-track layout, and that structural fact
carries the tradition rather than any single intensity choice. Passes `check_vector.py`.

## Hierarchy logic

Two devices. First, fixed zoning: the primary text occupies a larger, centred column and the gloss
occupies a narrower reserved outer zone, and the two never trade places. Second, relative scale
between the zones: the margin's text runs smaller and denser than the primary column's, so it reads
as present and legitimate but consistently subordinate, without ever needing to be hidden.

## Colour logic

Two roles historically, sometimes three. The primary ink for the main text, and a rubric or
secondary-colour ink reserved specifically for pointing — cross-references, corrections, insertion
marks — never for general emphasis or decoration. Where a third role exists it is a later
annotator's distinct ink, which is itself a kind of colour-coded authorship record.

## Rhythm

Two independent rhythms rather than one shared grid. The primary column keeps its own baseline
rhythm, set once. The margin keeps a looser rhythm generated by whenever an annotator actually wrote
something, so it runs crowded in some stretches and empty in others. The two rhythms are not meant
to reconcile; their mismatch is what shows the margin accumulated across time rather than being
laid out in one pass.

## Signature move

The margin visibly not aligned to the primary column's grid — its own rhythm, its own indentation,
sometimes its own hand, running independently down the page. Force the margin into strict alignment
with the primary text's grid and the layered-authority reading disappears; it stops looking like
accumulated commentary and starts looking like a designed sidebar.

## What it hands the system for free

A solved pattern for carrying a secondary content stream in permanent view alongside a primary one,
without demoting it into a drawer, tooltip, or footnote a reader has to open. It also hands a
built-in model for showing multiple authorship or provenance over time, useful anywhere a system
needs to show that more than one voice has touched a piece of content without erasing the earlier
ones.

## Type character

The primary text can run more formal and set. The marginal gloss runs in a more compressed register,
sometimes visibly a different hand from the main text, so the two tracks are distinguished by
density and register rather than strictly by size — this is a lineage where two coexisting type
characters do real work, not one flat register with position carrying everything.

## Voice

The primary text is formal and complete. The marginal voice is provisional and referential — "see,"
"cf.," a correction, a short gloss — closer to a note passed alongside the text than a finished
statement, and it is expected to read as many small, differently-timed voices rather than one
authored one.

## Failure mode

Forcing the margin into strict tabular alignment with the primary column, or hiding it until
interaction, erases the layered-authority reading this lineage exists to carry. Pushed toward more
grid complexity without a clear primary-versus-secondary distinction, the two tracks stop being
readable as commentary-alongside-text and start reading as plain clutter.

## What it cancels

Cancels a single unified content column as the only legitimate structure. Cancels the instinct to
hide secondary content in a drawer or tooltip by default. Cancels forced grid alignment between a
primary block and anything running alongside it, and cancels the assumption that a second content
stream must have arrived in one authorial pass.

## Behaviour pulled off home

Pull grid further toward unconventional and the two-track structure risks collapsing into a single
column read as decoration, since the primary-versus-margin distinction depends on the zoning staying
legible. Pull density up further and the marginal apparatus can start to outweigh the primary text,
inverting a hierarchy this tradition depends on holding the other way. Type violence can move either
direction without breaking the lineage, since the two-register distinction survives at both a quiet
and a louder setting.
