# 09-letterpress-broadside — Letterpress broadside

## Conditions

One urgent message that has to land in a single look, from a distance, with no return visit
expected. Maximum type contrast is the actual mechanism, not decoration on top of a message —
because the printer set the sheet from whatever wood and metal type blocks were in the shop that
day, the hierarchy is chaotic by construction and still has to read. The sheet is seen once, from
across a street, and then discarded.

## Home vector

`C3 T9 G7 S6 M1 D1` — passes `scripts/check_vector.py` (spread 8, two axes at or above 7 — type and
grid — and motion at 1 with density at 1 paying for type's extreme value). Type is the loud axis at
9: wildly different sizes, weights, and faces mixed on one sheet, because the shop's available type
determined the layout rather than a designed scale, and the result still has to resolve into a
readable order.

## Hierarchy logic

Not size alone, since size is already spent on chaos — the device that actually orders the sheet is
the horizontal rule. A broadside chunks into bands separated by rule lines, and the band nearest the
top carries the most urgent line by position, regardless of what type happened to be available for
it. The second device is enclosure of the single most critical line — a border or a rule drawn tight
around it — so it survives being read even when every other line is competing in size and weight.

## Colour logic

Usually two: black ink as structure, and one reserved accent, most often red, saved for exactly the
line that cannot be missed — a date, a warning, a reward figure. The ground is the paper itself,
typically cream or tan, uncorrected. The rule for the accent is absolute: it marks the single most
urgent fact on the sheet and nothing else, because a second use of it would split the reader's
attention the sheet cannot afford to lose.

## Rhythm

There is no modular grid rhythm; the repeating unit is the rule line itself, recurring every one to
three lines and chunking the sheet into bands. Each band's height is set by whichever type block
happened to be tallest, so the vertical rhythm is irregular by design — bound only by the convention
that a rule always separates one urgent claim from the next, never by a fixed measurement.

## Signature move

Deliberately mixing unrelated type sizes, weights, and faces on the same sheet, because that mixture
is what the original shop's available type produced and what the tradition's confidence depends on.
Replace the mixture with one consistent family at a designed scale and the sheet stops looking like a
broadside — it looks like a plain poster that lost its nerve.

## What it hands the system for free

An already-solved way to make one line unmissable without inventing a colour or size logic from
scratch: make it the physically largest thing on the sheet, full stop. It also hands permission to
let hierarchy look chaotic in the small and still resolve in the large, which most systems have to
earn through restraint this lineage never attempts.

## Type character

Drawn and mixed on purpose — contrast of era, weight, and face on one sheet, with display sizes
doing all the hierarchy work. Numerals are not tabular; this is an announcement, not a comparison,
and nothing here needs to align across entries.

## Voice

Urgent, declarative, plain. Short imperative lines, no hedging, no warmth — closer to a command than
to conversation, because the sheet has one chance to be read and no time to explain itself.

## Failure mode

Pushed into a persistent surface seen every day, the mixture that reads as urgent once becomes noise
on repeat viewing, because a returning reader has no fixed place to look — the hierarchy was
deliberately inconsistent from message to message, which only works when there is no second message
to compare it against. Pulled the other way, toward low type violence, the entire mechanism
disappears and what's left is a plain, forgettable poster.

## What it cancels

Cancels a single consistent type family or scale — inconsistency is the point, not an oversight to
fix. Cancels a persistent multi-visit navigation model, since the whole tradition assumes one look
and discard. Cancels a quiet, subtle accent colour; the accent has to compete at the same visual
volume as everything else on the sheet or it disappears entirely.

## Behaviour pulled off home

Type violence is the signature move itself, so it is the first thing that breaks the lineage if
pulled down — below roughly 6, the mixture reads as an ordinary poster rather than a broadside.
Grid unconventionality can flex further before the lineage disappears, because the rule-line bands
survive even a somewhat more ordered layout. What never survives a pull toward a persistent,
revisited surface is the assumption of one look: motion and density both have to stay near their
home values, because raising either turns the sheet into something meant to be read twice, which
this tradition was never built to reward.
