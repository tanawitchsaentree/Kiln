# 05-record-sleeve — Record sleeve

## Conditions

Identity and mood carrying more than function: one strong image or gesture dominates the whole
surface, meant to be recognized instantly and remembered, not read closely. A brief matches this
lineage when the real problem is being unmistakable across a room in a second, not conveying detail
that rewards a close read.

## Home vector

`C8 T7 G2 S5 M3 D1` — passes `scripts/check_vector.py` on its own; chroma at 8 is paid for by grid at
2 and density at 1. Chroma is the loud axis, deliberately, at 8: the entire point of a record sleeve
is that one striking colour or image choice is the identity, not an accent to it.

## Hierarchy logic

A single dominant image or gesture fills the frame, and everything else recedes around it — there is
no second competing focal point. Scale does the second job: the title or artist name is set large
enough to be read across a room at a glance, not for close reading at arm's length.

## Colour logic

Two to three roles, deliberately few: one dominant hue or image that is the identity, and a
supporting neutral for the small amount of text. The governing rule is that the dominant colour is
chosen once and never diluted by a second competing bold choice — the whole system's memorability
depends on there being exactly one loud colour decision, not several.

## Rhythm

There is no repeating module in the usual sense. The interval, such as it is, is compositional
balance around the one dominant gesture — tension and counterweight across the whole frame, not a
measurement that repeats at smaller scales.

## Signature move

One image or gesture filling the entire frame with nothing competing for attention, paired with
oversized display type legible from across a room. Dilute the image with competing detail, or shrink
the type back to reading scale, and the sleeve stops working — it becomes a poster with captions, not
an identity mark.

## What it hands the system for free

An instantly recognizable hero or identity moment with nothing left to invent: full-bleed imagery as
identity, paired with oversized display type, is a solved pairing for landing pages, brand moments,
and splash screens.

## Type character

A display face doing real, loud work at large sizes — drawn or expressive register is welcome, weight
and era contrast are intentional. Numerals are rarely load-bearing, since there is little tabular
content. Size itself is a hierarchy device at the largest scale this skill uses.

## Voice

Terse and iconic, mood before explanation: a title, a name, at most one line, with nothing
instructional attached. The register runs cool more often than warm, and never procedural.

## Failure mode

This lineage is identity-strong and application-weak. Pushed onto a dense product surface, it fails
immediately: the single-dominant-gesture hierarchy has no answer for a settings screen or a data
table, because its entire vocabulary is one thing winning while everything else recedes, and a table
needs many equally weighted things visible at once. The moment it has to hold a form with a dozen
fields, or a dashboard with several live numbers, it breaks — there is no second-tier hierarchy
device, no pattern for repeated rows, no calm mode to fall back to. It fails hard the moment it has
to hold a data table or a settings screen, because it was never built to hold that content, and
forcing it just produces a hero image sitting above an uncoordinated interface underneath.

## What it cancels

Dense tabular layouts, multiple simultaneous colour signals, small captioned type doing real
information work, and calm neutral surfaces as a default — everything here is built to be loud once
and remembered, not to sit quietly.

## Behaviour pulled off home

Push density up from 1 and the single-dominant-image logic breaks first, since a dominant gesture
only works with almost nothing else competing for the frame; past a small threshold the hierarchy
needs a second device this lineage does not supply, which is the tell that the brief has walked into
application territory it doesn't serve. Pull chroma down significantly and the signature move loses
its reason to exist, because the tradition's identity was never the composition alone — it was
always the boldness of the colour or image choice.
