# 18-painted-shop-sign — Painted shop sign

## Conditions

A storefront needs an identity mark made visibly by one person's hand, for one specific place, not
as a systemic identity meant to reproduce identically across locations. The warmth and idiosyncrasy
of the hand-painted mark is the actual selling point, not a flaw a system would normally correct
toward consistency. Match this lineage when the brief's real problem is a local, single-maker
identity, not any brand mark that merely looks casual or handwritten.

## Home vector

`C7 T6 G3 S8 M2 D1` — surface at 8 is the primary loud axis: visible brushwork, imperfect but
confident hand-lettering, a painted surface with real texture and slight asymmetry that reads as
made by a person. Chroma at 7 also crosses the loud threshold and is the secondary spend, on the one
bold hue the sign is built around — a barber pole red, a gold-leaf name. Two axes at 7 or above is
the concentration limit, and surface at 8 is paid for by motion at 2 and density at 1. Passes
`check_vector.py`.

## Hierarchy logic

Hierarchy comes from hand-drawn scale contrast and reserved colour, not a systematic grid. The
biggest word is painted biggest by eye, with weight and flourish varying stroke to stroke rather
than following a fixed ratio, and the sign's one dominant colour is reserved for the main word or
name so it reads as figure against whatever the storefront's ground colour is.

## Colour logic

Three roles at most. The storefront ground colour, the hand-lettered figure colour at high chroma —
often gold, red, or another single saturated hue that becomes the shop's de facto identity colour
with no formal brand guideline behind it — and sometimes a shadow or outline colour used purely to
hold legibility against a textured ground. The reserved figure colour is the one thing kept most
consistent across the sign, standing in for a brand system that was never formally designed.

## Rhythm

Generated by the maker's hand, not by a grid. Letter spacing holds "close enough" by eye rather than
by measurement, which means the interval that repeats is not a fixed unit but a visible, slight
variance from stroke to stroke and letter to letter — and that variance is not an error to correct,
it is the tell that a person, not a machine, made the mark.

## Signature move

Visible brushstroke variation within a single letterform: the same letter painted twice is never
pixel-identical, stroke thickness shifts along its length, edges are not perfectly clean. Remove
that variation and the letterform becomes vector type, at which point it stops being a painted sign
regardless of what shape the letters still take.

## What it hands the system for free

An immediate, legible "made by a person, in this place" signal, solved without needing any explicit
"local" or "handmade" messaging in the copy — the artefact itself proves the claim, so the system
never has to state it.

## Type character

Drawn, not set. A sign-painter's brush-lettering register: swelled strokes, hand-drawn serifs or
flourishes, confident but imperfect, with asymmetry expected rather than corrected. Never geometric,
never perfectly kerned — the character the type carries is the maker's hand, not a typeface's
consistency.

## Voice

Plain, warm, and local — "Fresh Bread Daily," a proprietor's own name — short and direct, closer to
something a person would say out loud on the street than to a written marketing line.

## Failure mode

Per `references/constraint.md`, this lineage is identity-strong and application-weak, and it fails
at scale and systemisation specifically: the whole point is one hand making one mark, and asking it
to become a repeatable component system means either repainting it by hand at every instance, which
breaks any notion of a scalable system, or vectorising it, which kills the texture and turns it into
a logo — the exact thing this lineage exists to not be.

## What it cancels

Cancels perfectly repeatable, identical component instances. Cancels vector-perfect kerning and
letterform consistency. Cancels any claim to being systemic or multi-location as a virtue, and
cancels flat, minimal colour roles in favour of one bold reserved hue that has to carry real weight.

## Behaviour pulled off home

Pull surface down toward flat — which is what happens when a brief tries to systemise this mark
across locations — and the hand-made signal disappears first; it is the single most fragile part of
this lineage, and once it is gone the mark reads as a generic bold-colour logo rather than a painted
sign. Chroma can drop somewhat without breaking the lineage, since a sign still reads as
hand-painted as long as the brushwork itself survives; surface is what this whole tradition actually
depends on holding.
