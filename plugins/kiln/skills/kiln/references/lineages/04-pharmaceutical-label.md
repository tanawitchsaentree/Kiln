# 04-pharmaceutical-label — Pharmaceutical label

## Conditions

Legally mandated information compressed into a small physical space, where nothing may be omitted
and zero tolerance exists for a missed warning. The space forces an extreme hierarchy discipline:
what gets boxed, what gets bolded, what runs at the smallest size that is still legally required to
be legible. A brief matches this lineage when the real problem is that scarcity, not when it is
merely "a lot of text in a small area."

## Home vector

`C3 T2 G3 S1 M1 D7` — passes `scripts/check_vector.py` on its own. Density is the loud axis at 7,
because the medium's problem is literal: legally mandated dosage, warnings, interactions, and lot
data all have to fit in a space set by the packaging, not by the designer.

## Hierarchy logic

Enclosure does the first job: a ruled box isolates the single most critical warning from the
surrounding text, regardless of how little room is left. A fixed weight-and-size floor does the
second: what is boldest is what is legally most critical, not what is most convenient to read — the
hierarchy answers to regulation before it answers to taste.

## Colour logic

Two or three roles at most: a white or near-white ground, black text, and, where used at all, one
reserved colour for the single most severe warning class. Colour is not the primary safety signal
here — containment and boxing are. The governing rule is restraint: the fewer colour roles, the less
chance a warning is missed because it was competing with something merely decorative.

## Rhythm

The interval is whatever is left over after every mandated element has been placed, not a rhythm
chosen for its own sake. Type size itself is fixed by the minimum legible size at the required
viewing distance, which regulation sets rather than the system.

## Signature move

The boxed, ruled enclosure holding the single most dangerous fact, isolated by a hard border from the
same-weight text around it, present no matter how tight the space gets. Remove the box and the
warning drowns in the paragraph around it; the label becomes a generic ingredient list, not a
pharmaceutical one.

## What it hands the system for free

A solved pattern for "what must never be missed" under real space pressure — a proven enclosure,
bold, and minimum-size discipline that transfers directly to any compliance-heavy screen: consent
text, risk disclosure, terms that must not be skimmed past.

## Type character

Small, upright, high x-height for legibility at minimum size. Bold is reserved for mandated severity,
never for taste. Numerals are tabular, since dosage and lot numbers must align. There are no display
sizes: type stays completely flat, and hierarchy exists only through weight and enclosure.

## Voice

Exact and plain. Instructions are imperative — take with food, do not exceed — and nothing that
matters is left out for the sake of brevity. There is no warmth in this register and no persuasion;
it is a compliance voice, not a brand voice.

## Failure mode

Pushed toward an identity or marketing surface where charm or brand warmth matters, the box-and-bold
compliance grammar reads as alarming and cold, and drains warmth from everything around it. Pushed
into a context with generous space, the logic has nothing to work against: its entire hierarchy
depends on scarcity, and given room to breathe it produces no ordinary visual hierarchy at all,
because it was never built to invent one.

## What it cancels

Decorative type, colour used for mood, generous optional whitespace as a hierarchy device, more than
a couple of type weights, and hierarchy expressed through size variety — size here is set by
regulation, not by design choice.

## Behaviour pulled off home

Pull density down from 7 and the boxed-warning move survives, but the discipline that gives it
meaning — pulling one fact out of a crowded field — has nothing left to pull it out of, and the
enclosure starts to look like arbitrary emphasis. Push type violence up and weight starts doing
decorative work instead of legal-severity work, which collapses the rule that boldness means risk
level rather than design taste.
